Almost everything in *The Lonely Pier* is blue, which is a way of saying almost everything is distance. The pier enters from the left on cross-braced legs and stops before it gets anywhere—structures like this are always promising to deliver you somewhere and never quite doing it.

I was after the particular quiet of a built thing at an unpopular hour. The water takes the pilings and breaks them into ribbons; the wave to the right is the only argument in the painting. A pier is a piece of human insistence stuck out into something that doesn't care, and at this hour you can see the whole negotiation at once.
